4 · Openings for you

Every day, ainews openings picks the week's new postings that fit you best, shared between established companies, startups and top-tier names, and sends them to the Telegram channel, each posting once. It makes no model call: the ranking uses the form the model already filled in for each posting (step 3), your skills list, and your preferences. This is step J3 on the architecture diagram.

Which postings are considered

A posting is a candidate when all of these hold:

  • Open, and read by the model. Without the model's form there are no skills to compare. New postings are read first, so a day's new postings are usually read the same day.
  • New: published in the last 7 days, by the employer's own date (else the day we first saw it).
  • Never sent before. Sent postings are recorded, so a posting reaches you once, however long it stays open.
  • Yours: its role family (as the model judged it from the duties) and at least one of its markets are in your lists, its level isn't one you skip, and it doesn't ask for more than your years plus 3.

Your preferences live in private/openings.yaml, which is gitignored. Copy config/openings.example.yaml to start; without the file these defaults apply:

The fit score

Each candidate gets a fit from 0 to 100, computed in code:

  • Skills, 70 points. Every skill the posting asks for has a weight: 1 if it's required, ½ if it's only preferred. A skill in your have list earns all of its weight, one in your learning list half, any other none. The points are 70 × earned ÷ total weight. A posting that lists no skills gets half the points: neither a match nor a miss.
  • Level, 30 points. A level in your levels list earns them all; otherwise a share: unknown 60%, mid 40%, manager 30%, any other 30%.

Three employer groups

Ranked by fit alone, most days would go to frontier labs and the best-known AI companies: their postings are written for exactly these skills, and they post a lot. Those are also the hardest offers to get. So every employer belongs to one of three groups, and each group has its own places in the day's list:

GroupWhoEmployersPlaces per list
🏢 EstablishedPublic companies, banks, insurers, pharma and consulting firms, and private companies past roughly 1,000 people: Salesforce, Capital One, TD, Roche, Datadog, Snowflake, CoreWeave…473
🌱 StartupsSmaller private companies: Legora, Dust, Faculty, LangChain, Baseten, Supabase…593
🏆 Top tierFrontier labs, the most sought-after AI companies and big-tech brands: Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Cohere, Thinking Machines Lab, Reflection AI, Isomorphic Labs, Physical Intelligence, Figure, Palantir, Sierra, Harvey, Glean, Perplexity, Cursor, Cognition, ElevenLabs, Midjourney, Scale AI, Databricks, Stripe, Figma and NVIDIA232
  • Places left empty move on. When a group has fewer good matches than places, the best matches from the other groups take them, so a quiet day for one group doesn't shorten the list.
  • Top tier never takes more than its 2, however many top-tier postings fit. A group set to 0 gets nothing, not even places left empty.
  • The group is the employer's, set by hand in config/companies.yaml (tier: top, established or startup). It's a judgment of how hard the employer is to get into, not a fact from the data: move an employer when its hiring bar feels different to you. Each employer's group is in the employer list.
  • Change the places with per_tier in private/openings.yaml, or for one run with --tier: ainews openings --tier top=0 leaves top tier out today.

From candidates to the day's list

  1. Best fit first, newer first among equal fits.
  2. One entry per role. The same title at the same employer in several cities is one entry, listing all its places; every posting in it counts as sent.
  3. At most 2 per employer, so one big employer can't fill a group.
  4. A fit of at least 50 (min_fit), whatever the group.
  5. Each group fills its places (3 established, 3 startups and 2 top tier), then empty places move on to the best of the rest, never to top tier. On a quiet day the list is shorter, and on a day with nothing above the bar nothing is sent.

What arrives in Telegram

A heading with how many postings were considered and how many picks each group got, then the openings under their group's heading (🏢 Established, 🌱 Startups, 🏆 Top tier, numbered straight through, best fit first in each). The eight usually take two messages; a message ends between groups, never inside one. The example above, rendered by the same code:

Skills %: how much of a posting's skills you cover, a skill you're learning at half; the gap is small from 80%, medium from 60%. Tap a title to open the posting. Each posting is sent once.
  • The title opens the employer's own posting.
  • The line under it: the employer, where (the first two cities, how many more, and the country's flag), the work policy, the level, and “visa sponsorship” when the posting says it offers it. Career sites write places in many ways (“USA, GA, Atlanta”, “California - San Francisco”); only the city is kept.
  • 💰 the posted pay, when there is one: the board's own figure if it publishes one, else the range in the text.
  • 🎯 the fit and when the posting was published.
  • 🧩 Skills how much of the posting's skills you cover, as a bar and a percentage: its required skills count in full and its nice-to-haves at half, and a skill you're learning earns half. Then the size of the gap (small from 80%, medium from 60%, big below) and how many required skills would be new to you.
  • ✅ You have the posting's skills on your “have” list, required ones first, and how many of the required ones that is.
  • ❌ New to you, required and ➖ New to you, nice to have: the posting's skills on neither of your lists.
  • 📚 Learning the posting's skills on your “learning” list.

📊 Where you stand closes the message, using the same measure for all of the week's roles for you. Each role counts once, including ones sent before. It shows how much of a typical role's skills you cover (the median), how many roles have a small, medium or big gap, which of your skills they ask for most, and which skills you don't have they ask for most (the share of roles that list each). The 🚀 line names the three skills that would add the most to your coverage, required ones before nice-to-haves, and how many roles would have a small gap if you learned them. Each list keeps these numbers (in reports.stats), so you can follow your gap over time.

These lines show anyone who can read the channel which skills you have and which you lack, as the weekly report's “Your fit” section does.

Sent once: how it's tracked

  • Building isn't sending. ainews openings saves the message (and out/openings-<date>.md) without sending. Postings count as sent only once the message goes out with --send or ainews send --openings, so a preview never uses them up.
  • The record: the job_alerts table holds every posting sent, with its fit and the report it went out in.
  • Unread postings: when new postings in your roles still wait for the model, the heading says how many. They become candidates after the next ainews jobs run reads them, as long as they're still within 7 days.
  • Cleaning the channel: ainews tg-clean keeps the latest openings, brief and jobs report, and removes older posts and the earlier copy of openings sent again with --redo.

The daily routine

CommandWhat it does
ainews jobsPolls the employers and lets the model read new postings (steps 1–3)
ainews openingsRanks the candidates, prints the list with each fit, and saves the message; nothing is sent
ainews send --openingsSends the message you just checked
ainews openings --sendBuilds and sends in one go
ainews openings --redo --sendRebuilds the latest sent openings, say after a layout change, and sends them again; without --send their postings count as unsent until you send
ainews openings --tier top=0No top-tier openings this time; --tier startup=5 gives startups more places. The rest keep their places

Free and instant: no model call, no network call except to Telegram. Deployed, the 05:00 jobs run (every three days) sends the openings at the end (ainews jobs --send-openings).

Why not let the model rank? The model already did the hard part, reading each posting into a form. Ranking those forms in code costs nothing, gives the same answer every run, and shows its working: every opening lists the skills behind its fit, so you can see why it's there and fix your lists if it shouldn't be.
