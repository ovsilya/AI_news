How the job search works

Every day the jobs lane (lane ② on the architecture diagram) collects open AI engineering jobs straight from the employers' own career sites. It keeps the ones in your target roles and markets, and has a model read each one into a fixed set of facts: skills, seniority, pay and remote policy. Once a week, code turns those facts into a report on what employers ask for, what they pay, who is hiring, and how your skills match.

Numbers on these pages are a snapshot from 13 Oct 2026, the second day of tracking. Lists and limits are generated from the code and config, so they are always current.

The idea in four sentences

  1. Go to the source, employer by employer. Postings come from the own job systems of a fixed list of employers ({{n_list}} on Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby, {{n_workday}} on Workday), never from LinkedIn or Indeed, whose terms forbid programs. An employer that isn't on the list isn't seen; searching by role is planned.
  2. Keep only what's yours. A title filter keeps your role families and a location filter keeps the US, Canada, UK, Switzerland, UAE and remote jobs.
  3. Read each posting once. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite fills in the same form for every posting, and the form is read again only if the posting's text changes.
  4. Count in code, explain with the model. Every number in the report is computed from the data. The model writes the commentary, and any sentence with a number that isn't in the data is removed.

The six steps

Steps 1–3 run every day (ainews jobs); steps 4–6 run once a week (ainews weekly). Click a step to read how it works.

Colours as on the architecture diagram: orange is outside the project, white is plain code, purple is a model call, green is storage.

From 13,800 postings to one report

How much each step keeps, on 13 Oct 2026 (bars to scale):

12,948 open postings listed by the {{n_list}} job-board APIs (every job at those employers)
885 search results with AI titles on the {{n_workday}} Workday sites (only AI keywords are searched)
2,275 kept: in a role family and in your markets (1,591 + 684)
210 read by the model so far; the other 2,065 wait their turn, up to 200 a day
180 of those are open and in your {{n_targets}} target families, so they count in the current report

The report grows as the backlog is read: about ten daily runs until all 2,275 have been read. After that, each day only adds the new postings.

Words used on these pages

Posting
One job advert on an employer's career site. The same job in two cities can be two postings.
Job board / ATS
The system an employer uses to publish jobs and take applications (an applicant tracking system). Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby are popular with startups; Workday with banks, insurers, pharma and big tech.
Tenant, career site
On Workday, each employer is a tenant (nvidia) with one or more career sites (NVIDIAExternalCareerSite).
Role family
The kind of job, such as FDE / applied AI or ML engineer. Your {{n_targets}} target families are {{targets}}.
Market
Where the job is: USA, Canada, UK, Switzerland, UAE, or remote.
Poll
One visit to every career site to see what's open today.
Diff
Comparing today's postings with what is stored, to find new, changed and closed ones.
Extraction
The model reading a posting and filling in the fixed form (skills, seniority, pay…).
Canonical skill
One agreed name per skill, so “k8s” and “Kubernetes” are counted together.
Share
The percentage of postings that ask for something: “Python 73%” means 73 of every 100 target postings name Python.
Median
The middle value when numbers are sorted: half are above, half below. Used for pay so a few extreme salaries don't skew it.
Confidence interval
The range a true share probably lies in, given how many postings we saw. Used to tell a real rise in demand from noise.
Coverage
How much of the demand for the top skills your skills cover, weighted by how often each skill is asked for.

What it never does

  • No scraping of job sites that forbid it. LinkedIn, Indeed, jobs.ch, Bayt and Naukrigulf ban automated access, so they are never visited. Planned: your own job-alert emails from them, read from a Gmail label.
  • No logins and no getting around blocks. Only public endpoints, the same ones the career page itself uses, and only parts of a site that its robots.txt allows.
  • Your personal data stays private. Your skills list (private/skills.yaml) and profile are gitignored and never in the public repo. These pages explain the fit calculation with made-up numbers.
  • No guessed numbers. If a number in the model's commentary isn't in the statistics, the sentence is dropped.
