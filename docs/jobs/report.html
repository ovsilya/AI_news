5 · The weekly report and your fit

Once a week, ainews weekly turns the filled-in forms into a report: which roles are open where, the skills employers ask for, what they pay, who is hiring most, and how your skills cover the demand. Every number is computed in code. The model writes three short notes on what the numbers mean, and code removes any sentence whose numbers aren't in the statistics. These are steps W1–W3 on the architecture diagram.

Which postings count

  • Only postings the model has read. The family, skills and pay come from the form. Until the backlog is read, the report covers fewer postings than are stored: on 13 Oct 2026 it was 180 of 2,275.
  • Only open postings, except for “new this week”, “closed this week” and the rising and falling skills, which look at when postings were published.
  • The model's family, not the title's guess. Your 5 target families are FDE / applied AI, Solutions engineer, LLM / GenAI engineer, AI engineer, ML engineer. ML research and data science are shown as “Also open”, and postings the model files as “other” are left out.
  • These employers, not the whole market. The report says so, and the model is told not to present the numbers as market-wide.

Rising and falling skills, explained

Is a skill really in more demand, or did we just see a few more postings that mention it? Each week compares two groups of target postings:

  • This week: postings published in the last 7 days.
  • Before: postings published in the 4 weeks before that (days 8–35).

For each skill, both shares get a 95% confidence interval (a Wilson interval), the range the true share probably lies in given how many postings we saw. Fewer postings mean a wider range. A skill counts as rising only if this week's whole range sits above the earlier range, and falling only if it sits below. Overlapping ranges mean “can't tell yet”, and nothing is claimed.

Example. A skill named in 24 of 60 postings this week (40%, interval 29–53%) and in 30 of 200 before (15%, interval 11–21%): the ranges don't overlap, so it's rising.
With 9 of 40 this week (22.5%, interval 12–38%), the share still looks higher, but the range overlaps 11–21%, so no claim is made.

Three safety rules: each group needs at least 30 postings; a skill needs at least 5 mentions on the side that's moving; and nothing is reported until 14 days after tracking started (13 Oct 2026, so from 27 Oct). Why the wait: on the first days, the “before” group holds only older postings that were still open when we started. Those are the hard-to-fill roles, not a fair sample, and comparing them with this week would invent trends.

Your fit, explained

You keep two lists in private/skills.yaml, which is gitignored and never leaves your machine: skills you have (used in production or shown in a project) and skills you're learning. The report compares them with the market in two ways.

A typical open role comes first. For every open role in your target families, whatever its date or level, each role once (the same title at one employer in several cities is one role), it measures how much of that role's own skills you cover, as the openings do: required skills in full, nice-to-haves at half, a skill you're learning at half. The report gives the median, the median for technical skills only (leaving out customer-facing ones such as “Communication” or “Consulting”), how many roles have a small gap (80% or more), and the median per role family. This is the honest measure of your gap. The openings sent during the week are the best fits by design, so they always look better: they say so, and compare themselves with a typical role.

A made-up example: “A typical open role: you cover 48% of its skills, 44% of its technical ones; 9% of the 600 open roles have a small gap (80%+). By role: FDE / applied AI 61% · Solutions engineer 57% · AI engineer 45% · ML engineer 33%.”

The top 30 skills come next, which shows which common skills you have and which you lack. This coverage is weighted by demand: a skill named in 70% of postings counts 3½ times as much as one named in 20%. It is usually higher than a typical role's, because the most-asked skills are the common ones, and most gaps sit in the long tail of less common skills that this list leaves out.

The gaps are the top skills you don't have, biggest share first, marked “(learning)” if you're on it and “↑” if the skill is rising. In the example: System design 50% (learning), Kubernetes 20%, PyTorch 20%. The model's “Comment from a pro” then picks one or two gaps to close first and suggests a small project and how to show it.

The notes: written by the model, checked by code

Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite gets the statistics as JSON (not the postings) and the prompt prompts/weekly.md (version weekly-v4). It writes a headline and three notes, each an intro plus 3–4 labelled bullets:

  • Market (💬 What it means): the busiest roles and markets, how remote and customer-facing the work is, who's hiring, pay.
  • Skills (💬 What it means): the core skills, what sets FDE and applied AI roles apart, and what's rising or falling. Before trends are ready, it says so once and describes current demand instead.
  • Fit (🎓 Comment from a pro): written as your supervisor, a senior GenAI/ML engineer, in a one-to-one. Labels: Focus, Learn, Try (a small project to ship this month), Show it (how to present it in a CV, portfolio or interview), Career.

The prompt's rules: use only numbers from the JSON, written the same way; calculate nothing new (no differences or sums); quote pay ranges exactly (“$180k–$248k”); don't present the numbers as market-wide; use plain words, with no hype, emojis or exclamation marks.

The number check. Every sentence and bullet in the notes, and the headline, is scanned for numbers. Each number must appear in the statistics JSON. Formatting is ignored, so “0.30” and “0.3” match. A sentence with even one unsupported number is dropped, and ainews weekly prints a warning naming it. So the model can explain, but it can't invent.

Publishing

  • Always saved locally: out/jobs-<date>.md, and a row in the reports table with the statistics it was written from, the text, and the cost (about 1 cent).
  • Posted only when you say so: ainews weekly --send builds and posts in one go; ainews send --report posts the latest saved report. It's packed into as few Telegram messages as fit (each up to 4,096 characters), and only the first makes a sound.
  • The footer says where the data comes from: how many employers on which systems, and the run's cost.
